JCGM 100:2008 — Evaluation of Measurement Data — Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (the GUM)

Section titled “JCGM 100:2008 — Evaluation of Measurement Data — Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (the GUM)”

Family: ISO · Published by: Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology (JCGM) — co-sponsored by BIPM, IEC, IFCC, ILAC, ISO, IUPAC, IUPAP, OIML. Identical to ISO/IEC Guide 98-3:2008. · Standard version: 2008 · Current edition: JCGM 100:2008 (GUM 1995 with minor corrections) · Adopted by jinflow: 2026-07-14

Jurisdiction: global

What it is

Section titled “What it is”

The GUM is the definitive metrology standard for expressing measurement uncertainty. Every metrologist worldwide speaks its vocabulary: standard uncertainty (u, one standard deviation in the measurand’s units), expanded uncertainty (U = k·u where k is the coverage factor — typically 2 for ~95% coverage), combined uncertainty (from multiple sources via root-sum-of-squares under uncorrelated assumption), and coverage interval. jinflow’s uncertainty contract (see docs/design/uncertainty_first_class.md) speaks GUM’s standard uncertainty: every Reading’s uncertainty field is 1σ in that reading’s own units. This lets riverflow’s uncertainty output interoperate with WMO Hydrological Observing System (WHOS), USGS Water Data, and every WaterML 2.0 consumer that expects GUM semantics. jinflow does NOT emit expanded uncertainty (U) or combine uncertainty from multiple sources — packs pick the dominant contributor via the uncertainty_source enum instead.

Our conformance

Section titled “Our conformance”

Status: Referenced · First release: 0.56.2

Entity mapping

Section titled “Entity mapping”
  • standard uncertainty (u) → <Reading-family>.uncertainty column Every Reading row (RawReading, Measurement, EnvironmentalVariable) carries an uncertainty field expressed as one standard deviation (1σ) in the reading’s own units. GUM 4.2.3 (u = s(qk), the experimental standard deviation of the mean) is the direct semantic. Values are non-negative real; nullable when unknown.
  • type A vs type B evaluation → <Reading-family>.uncertainty_source enum GUM Section 4 distinguishes Type A (statistical from a series of observations) from Type B (from prior data, judgment, specifications). jinflow’s uncertainty_source enum (calibration_model | calibration_drift | fouling | sensor_stuck | network_gap | vendor_datasheet | unknown) is a domain-adapted Type A/B mix: calibration_model is Type A from the fit; vendor_datasheet is Type B; fouling / calibration_drift / sensor_stuck / network_gap are Type B based on physical argument. jinflow does not require packs to declare A vs B explicitly — the enum value implies it.
  • attribution of uncertainty to a source → <Reading-family>.uncertainty_source_ref Optional FK to the row that dominates a reading’s uncertainty (a Calibration id, a MaintenanceEvent id). This is the “why?” traversal — the GUM standard emphasises that every uncertainty must be traceable to its source; jinflow makes the traceability clickable.

Features supported

Section titled “Features supported”
  • Per-reading standard uncertainty (u) as GUM 4.2.3 defines it — one σ in the measurand’s units
  • Non-negative real domain, nullable when unknown (per GUM’s guidance that unknown uncertainties should not be silently zeroed)
  • Source attribution via uncertainty_source enum + uncertainty_source_ref pointer (traceability)
  • Interoperation with WaterML 2.0’s om:resultQuality field (GUM-adjacent)
  • Interoperation with SensorThings resultQuality via the same field

Not covered

Section titled “Not covered”
  • Expanded uncertainty (U = k·u) — jinflow emits u only. Consumers who need U compute it downstream with their own coverage factor.
  • Combined uncertainty from multiple sources — jinflow picks the dominant contributor via the enum rather than summing squares. A future refinement could add a uncertainty_contributions JSON array carrying the per-source components.
  • Coverage factor (k) declaration on individual readings — implicit k=1 (standard uncertainty). No k=2 (expanded) fields.
  • Degrees of freedom (ν) — not carried on any reading. Would be needed for a rigorous k derivation via the Welch–Satterthwaite formula.
  • GUM’s Type A / Type B classification as explicit metadata — implicit via the uncertainty_source enum.
  • Monte-Carlo evaluation (JCGM 101:2008 supplement) — out of scope.
  • Correlated uncertainty propagation (GUM 5.2) — the pipeline treats sources as independent; correlated sources would need explicit modelling.

Other languages

Section titled “Other languages”

Deutsch

Section titled “Deutsch”

Der GUM ist der massgebende metrologische Standard für die Angabe der Messunsicherheit. Jeder Metrologe weltweit spricht sein Vokabular: Standardunsicherheit (u, eine Standardabweichung in den Einheiten der Messgrösse), erweiterte Unsicherheit (U = k·u, wobei k der Erweiterungsfaktor ist — typischerweise 2 für ~95% Überdeckung), kombinierte Unsicherheit (aus mehreren Quellen über Quadratsummenwurzel bei unkorrelierter Annahme) und Überdeckungsintervall. Der Unsicherheitsvertrag von jinflow (siehe docs/design/uncertainty_first_class.md) spricht die Standardunsicherheit des GUM: Das Feld uncertainty jeder Messung ist 1σ in den eigenen Einheiten dieser Messung. Damit kann die Unsicherheitsausgabe von riverflow mit dem WMO Hydrological Observing System (WHOS), USGS Water Data und jedem WaterML-2.0-Konsumenten interoperieren, der GUM-Semantik erwartet. jinflow gibt KEINE erweiterte Unsicherheit (U) aus und kombiniert keine Unsicherheit aus mehreren Quellen — Packs wählen stattdessen den dominierenden Beitrag über die uncertainty_source-Enumeration.

Français

Section titled “Français”

Le GUM est le standard métrologique de référence pour exprimer l’incertitude de mesure. Tous les métrologues du monde parlent son vocabulaire : incertitude-type (u, un écart-type dans les unités du mesurande), incertitude élargie (U = k·u où k est le facteur d’élargissement — typiquement 2 pour ~95% de couverture), incertitude composée (à partir de plusieurs sources via la racine de la somme des carrés en hypothèse non corrélée) et intervalle de couverture. Le contrat d’incertitude de jinflow (voir docs/design/uncertainty_first_class.md) parle l’incertitude-type du GUM : le champ uncertainty de chaque lecture est 1σ dans les unités propres de cette lecture. Cela permet à la sortie d’incertitude de riverflow d’interopérer avec le WMO Hydrological Observing System (WHOS), USGS Water Data et tout consommateur WaterML 2.0 attendant la sémantique du GUM. jinflow n’émet PAS d’incertitude élargie (U) ni ne combine l’incertitude de plusieurs sources — les packs choisissent le contributeur dominant via l’énumération uncertainty_source.

Italiano

Section titled “Italiano”

Il GUM è lo standard metrologico definitivo per esprimere l’incertezza di misura. Ogni metrologo nel mondo parla il suo vocabolario: incertezza tipo (u, una deviazione standard nelle unità del misurando), incertezza estesa (U = k·u dove k è il fattore di copertura — tipicamente 2 per ~95% di copertura), incertezza combinata (da più fonti tramite la radice della somma dei quadrati sotto ipotesi non correlata) e intervallo di copertura. Il contratto di incertezza di jinflow (vedi docs/design/uncertainty_first_class.md) parla l’incertezza tipo del GUM: il campo uncertainty di ogni lettura è 1σ nelle unità proprie di quella lettura. Questo permette all’output di incertezza di riverflow di interoperare con il WMO Hydrological Observing System (WHOS), USGS Water Data e ogni consumatore WaterML 2.0 che si aspetta la semantica del GUM. jinflow NON emette incertezza estesa (U) né combina l’incertezza da più fonti — i pack scelgono il contributore dominante tramite l’enumerazione uncertainty_source.
