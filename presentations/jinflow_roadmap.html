jinflow · a jazzisnow productRoadmap2026-09-08

The next sixteen weeks

jinflow is a declarative analytical engine: you declare what should be detected — a signal, a thesis, a verdict — and the engine compiles it, runs it against the data, and hands back a single portable knowledge store you can browse, ship or freeze.

What this document is. A capability plan to 31 December, written from the working plan the team actually uses, with the same dates and the same confidence markers. It states what is running today, what is scheduled, and what is a bet.

What it is not. It makes no commercial claims — no pipeline, no revenue, no pricing. There are no customers yet, and the plan says so wherever that matters.

What already runs. The engine, the browser surface, the command line and a sovereign proxy mode; five deployment topologies from a single laptop to a cloud build; identity and per-tenant access control; a packaged macOS application; and real data — including a Swiss hospital group's anonymised ERP export and a cantonal agriculture dataset — carried through the whole pipeline into a shipped store.

What is missing is not a feature. It is that everything above currently depends on one person being reachable: which tenants a machine serves, what may be asked of it, and what to type when somebody joins are all knowledge rather than system. The four phases that follow are, in order, how that stops being true.

04Evidence

What closes each phase — checkable, not asserted

Each phase carries a done-when that names evidence rather than a feeling. These are the four, in the words the working plan uses:

  • Harden. Every critical finding is either closed with a test that fails without the fix, or accepted in writing, with a reason and a name against it.
  • Operable. A person who is not the founder can — from the documents alone — install, register a tenant, invite a colleague, publish a tenant, take it dark again, and say which version is running. Without asking anyone.
  • Extensible & connectable. An outside agent holding only a pass can discover and read one tenant over MCP; and that same tenant gains a new finding while its source data is nowhere near it.
  • In someone else's hands. Somebody outside this project completes a real task on their own data without asking us a question.

And what we are deliberately not doing: no eleventh domain pack, no Windows or Linux packaging this year, no multi-master federation, no billing system, and no engine migration. Each was considered and each is out.

05Stated openlySecurity

We ran an adversarial review against ourselves, and it found things

On 7 September an adversarially-verified review covered the whole codebase — 11 subsystem slices, four lenses, every candidate finding independently re-tested before it counted. 29 findings were confirmed: 7 critical, 11 high, 9 medium, 2 low; 15 of them security.

Why it is on this page. Because the alternative reads worse. A pre-customer product with no findings has not been looked at; a product with findings and a scheduled repayment has been. The fixes cluster into three shared causes rather than 29 separate ones, which is the difference between a rewrite and a fortnight.

What has been decided. The work was postponed deliberately — no customers, therefore no customer data at stake — and scheduled as a lockout: a dedicated pass at the end of September, fixing the class rather than the instance. The single highest-value mitigation needs no code and is being applied now.

The report itself stays internal until the lockout closes: it contains working detail against live surfaces, and publishing that before the fix would be the one genuinely irresponsible option.

06How this gets built

One founder, and a way of working that is itself the product thesis

1human author
7 mofrom first commit to production

The engine is built by one person working with several AI agent sessions in parallel git worktrees, each holding a lane — identity, the analytical surface, the cloud substrate, typology and cartography, ingestion — merging through a scripted round trip. Every number above is in the repository history and can be checked line by line.

The bottleneck is not throughput, and we can prove it. One unruled design question has held a finished piece of work for weeks while four hundred commits landed around it. That is a useful thing to know about a plan: it is paced by decisions, not by hands — which is exactly why this roadmap names four decisions and their dates rather than a longer list of features.
