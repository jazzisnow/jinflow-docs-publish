What is a fleet?

Sense 62 was written to stop one word doing two jobs. It is now doing four, and a fifth use arrived last week.

This deck is the shared page for Mig, sister‑9 and sister‑1 — the argument so far, the definition that arrived, a fixed vocabulary, and what is still open.

It is no longer an agenda. Earlier versions said “nothing here is settled”, which stopped being true the moment the fleet was defined — so the next slide says what is settled and by whose authority, before anything argues.

02The method

Not “what is a fleet” first — what jobs is the word doing?

Define one thing while using it for four and you will define it correctly and keep the confusion. So the jobs come first, and the useful question is which of them actually need the same noun.

The jobWhereStatus
J1Authority scope — what a fleet‑admin may operate requestCanFleetShipped
J2Observability object — the population, and where each member lives Sense 62Modelled, not built
J3Credential boundary — key at rest, App key, substrate creds Resolver §9Ruled 2026‑08‑11
J4Address — the thing you broadcast to hosts.yml, the footing, the served set, the access policy Shipped four times

J4 was never undefined. It has existed all along under another word — which is why the address facts sit in four homes today, and any two of them can disagree. (s9)

03ConsequenceJ3

A credential boundary is a place. A fleet is not.

“Anyone running jinflow on their own infrastructure is their own fleet.”Resolver §9 — location-scoped by construction
“Operator-scoped, not location-scoped. A view, not a place.”Sense 62 — the definition it contradicts

A view cannot hold a key. So this is not a coincidence that will end at the first hybrid customer — it is a category error already, and decidable now.

This is no longer a ruling the deck is waiting on. Once the fleet is the contents of a register, and a register lives somewhere, J3 is the installation — it follows from the definition rather than needing its own decision (slide 01). What the slide keeps is the argument, because the argument is what makes the consequence safe to nod at.

The consequence was written as: fleet_id goes on every row — as a population it means whose these are, as a deployment where they run — so it should be born an installation id rather than renamed once rows exist. The register definition arrived after that sentence and changes it, which this deck did not reconcile until Mig caught it: if membership is constituted by being in the book, a column repeating it is constant within a store and becomes a second home for a boundary fact. See slide 11 for the narrow form, and the condition under which the column comes back.

04The three kinds

Membership cardinality is the aspect

Three qualifiers on one noun was the signal. The analysis says they were never three flavours of one thing: they differ in what they contain, and in how many of them a tenant can be in at once.

WasShapeA tenant belongs toWhy it must be so
ofleeta treeexactly one leaf, plus ancestors single parenthood is what makes it an organisation rather than a tagging scheme
pfleeta treeone place per presence — and a tenant may have several a thing runs somewhere. Two places at once is not one tenant twice; it is two presences
lfleeta setmany, overlapping arbitrary by design — the freedom is the point

Two things fall out. Sense 62’s flat vs nested facet split is the wrong axis — two of these are trees and behave nothing alike. And its open question about multi‑presence dissolves: the tenant is the identity; only presences have a place.

05ProposedVocabulary

Two of the three already had names. We were shadowing them.

pfleet

Site + Presence. Operating Modes already says a Site is where a tenant instance lives. A “pfleet of presences” is just the presences at a site.

ofleet

Organisation. A tree with single parenthood is what that word means. Calling it a fleet added nothing and cost the word its precision.

lfleet → Cohort

The only genuinely new thing: a curated, overlapping set. Grouped for a purpose — and “a tenant is in several cohorts” reads naturally.

Dropping the kinds restores the word: there is one fleet, and the three “kinds” were three classifications over it — which is what Sense 62 called facets. What the restored word still lacks is a boundary. The next two slides take the old one away and supply a better one.

06A correction

“Operator” was the authority scope in disguise

Sense 62 needed to say a fleet is not defined by where, so it reached for whose. But scoping by an actor is J1 — the very job the paper opens by insisting we separate from J2.

So the paper says “the word does two jobs — separate them”, then defines the second in terms of the first, under a word chosen so it does not look like it is doing that.

Drop it and nothing is lost. What varies by asker is the view, and “which tenants may I see?” is already answered, and already shipped.

But it takes the boundary with it. Operator was the only thing bounding “all your tenants, wherever they live.” Without it the fleet means every tenant anywhere that runs jinflow — which is not a set anyone can hold, address, or display. Something has to replace it, or the word does die here.

07ProposedThe definition

The register is the boundary

“The fleet is defined through all ventures that are registered in the fleet store.”Mig, 2026-08-31

Definition by registration — the way the word has always worked for ships. A fleet is what is on the register. That answers the slide before it: the fleet is not a view over an unbounded world, it is the contents of a book, and the book is a file in a place.

So it does not refine Sense 62’s definition — it replaces it. A register lives somewhere, which makes the fleet installation‑scoped: the exact opposite of “operator‑scoped, not location‑scoped; a view, not a place.” One of the two has to go, and only one of them can be built.

The cost, and it is the dangerous class. Sense 62 §3.2 requires the roster to stay a projection — a disk scan, never stored, because “a stored projection is a second source of truth.” A register is authoritative. Those two collide head‑on.

The fix: register presences, not tenants. A tenant on disk is a fact you discover — store it and you have duplicated the disk. “This installation serves tenant X” is a decision somebody made, and decisions are exactly what a register is for. The book never shadows the scan, and the drift cannot run the dangerous way: an unregistered tenant is not missing from the fleet, it is not served — which is both true and safe.

Proposed name   The store becomes the FLS — the Fleet Store, sibling to AFS, KLS and OPS. It does not store the fleet; it constitutes it. (Mig, 2026-08-31)

08ProposedThe installation

The book decides who belongs. The reader only decides what it knows.

jin serve makes it cheap to run several engines against one book on one machine — which is Mig's question, and the one that turns the definition into something buildable: how many engines share an FLS?

If the FLS constitutes the fleet, two registers on one machine are two fleets — and an engine cannot be in a fleet it disagrees about. So: one FLS per installation. Which finally makes the word pointable rather than hand‑wavy: an installation is the set of engines that share one FLS. Defining the abstract term from a file at a path is the right direction — it is what replaces the boundary that operator took with it.

Membership is by the book you write to, not the book you read. A JinServer on another machine cannot share a file; it reads a replica, and a replica is a copy, not the register. So strictly it is not a member — it is a client of the installation. And the book loses nothing by it, because the book never recorded engines in the first place: it records presences — a tenant, at a site — and a remote JinServer is one of the things that hosts one. No second concept is needed. (s9 — the crack, kept rather than patched; the engine / presence distinction is s1's)

And staleness never touches membership: a stale footing makes an engine ignorant, never an ex‑member. That is §11's availability / exposure split arriving in a third place.

The writer   Not a daemon. The fleet tier must degrade to nothing — one grower, one tenant, offline, on a laptop. A component that must be running before an operator can register a tenant makes the tier a prerequisite, which is the one thing it must never be. So writes go through jin fleet verbs and SQLite/WAL is the lock — the OPS precedent exactly: human‑paced writes, many readers, one writer. One code rule, not a component: a write is one transaction, never a read‑modify‑write spanning process boundaries. It is safe rather than lucky because WAL does not serialise over a network share and does not have to — remote engines never write. The constraint and the mechanism share a boundary, which is worth saying out loud so nobody later “improves” this by putting the FLS on a share. (s9)

09J4 — address and delivery

The village, and the houses

“The fleet itself is not tangible for postmen… it’s like a village. But every house in the village gets the message.”Mig, 2026-08-30

An address is a resolvable expression, not an object — so a fleet can be a view and still be addressable. The analogy also splits two things that had been welded together, and the awkward cases then answer themselves:

IsConsequence
The addressa Cohort, an Organisation, or a Site curated, declared, or computed membership
The recipienta presence — the house a tenant has no inbox. Only a running server receives
No presencea plot with no house addressed, undeliverable — recorded, never a silent success
Two presencestwo houses on one plot both delivered; idempotency is the house’s problem

What the analogy does not remove: which village. An organisation and a cohort can share a name and resolve to different houses — and the operational one (everything running here) looks identical in the grammar to the harmless one. The aspect must be spoken, never inferred. That is not a new rule; it is P18 applied to blast radius.

10Ruled & built2026-09-08

Routing carries {pack, tenant} — and nothing else

The tenant access policy — shipped — moved visibility to the tenant. Its thesis sentence is “the host stops deciding policy,” written because one tenant on two hosts had two personalities. The routing citizen re-created exactly that, and this deck proposed a split: drop public, keep require_auth.

The ruling went further, and the second half of our proposal was wrong. Both fields leave. public because a security bit with three homes eventually fails OPEN — the tenant's access policy and its authored kind already hold it. And require_auth because it gates a host's landing page: what the host does when you arrive with no target. That is not routing at all, so it stays in host config, ships with the image, and never enters the store.

If the same tenant is served by two hosts, must the answer be the same? Yes → the tenant owns it. No → the host owns it. Routing owns neither.The test the ruling leaves behind — so the next one is cheap

The argument that settled it was one row of the live file, not the doc. app.jinflow.io and this-is.jinflow.io serve no tenant at all and disagree on require_auth. A flag present where there is no tenant cannot be a tenant property — and is not routing either. (s9 audited the file rather than reasoning from the paper.)

The consequence lands in §11: with nothing permissive in the footing, the fail‑CLOSED half is gone — a stale replica cannot widen access, only make an engine ignorant. Availability, the fail‑OPEN axis, is untouched. Nobody builds a staleness horizon and nobody sets a dial.

And the 404 that the type could not express is now a fourth outcome. Route had two — unknown host, or resolved — so a host pointing at a tenant this installation does not serve printed a healthy row while 404‑ing to everyone, its owner included. It now has four: resolved · unknown‑host (fail‑safe) · no‑presence (loud misconfiguration, counted in the footer) · peer‑link. Against the real host file it flags seven hosts. A box that cannot tell reports unknown rather than absence — a guard that cries wolf gets muted.

Two stores, specced independently by two papers that did not cite each other. This deck called it “one build away from the worst available outcome”. Sister‑3's correction is sharper in both directions: overstated, because the seam turned out to be a scope boundary and those do not drift the way taste boundaries do; and understated, because until an organisation carries a declared external reference there is no way to say two installations hold one organisation — and that gap is invisible rather than loud. Resolved 2026‑09‑10 (slide 12).

Still live: fleet grants in two homes, shipped in one and proposed again in the other — and the seam gives it a new edge she has flagged rather than ruled: a grant scoped to an org node is durable across a rebuild, a grant scoped to system is authority over one installation. They share a column today and now want different homes.

11Vocabulary

The words, once

Every term the deck now relies on, in one place — so the argument can be checked against a fixed vocabulary rather than a drifting one.

TermMeans
Fleetthe ventures registered in the FLS. Bounded by the register, and therefore enumerable, addressable, and installation‑scoped
FLSthe Fleet Store — the register, plus what this installation serves and how it reaches it. Sibling to AFS · KLS · OPS
Installationone sovereign deployment: its own keys, its own FLS. What Resolver §9 was calling a fleet
Tenantthe isolation atom — a sealed cell. (Venture, parked, is the same atom under a better word)
Presencea tenant running at a Site. What gets registered, what gets addressed, and the only thing that receives
Sitewhere presences live — laptop, cloud, bucket
Faceta classification over the fleet — that is, over the members of one register. It assigns every member a value; it never connects them. Identified by its membership cardinality, not its shape. Its extent is one FLS; whether its content is stored there is the open seam (slide 12)
Organisationthe exactly‑one facet — a single‑parent tree. Was ofleet
Cohortthe many, overlapping facet — a curated set, grouped for a purpose. Was lfleet
Addressa resolvable expression naming recipients — not an object. A Cohort, an Organisation, or a Site

Retired by this deck: ofleet · pfleet · lfleet — three cardinalities, not three flavours · operator-scoped — the authority scope in disguise · fleet_id — see below.

Narrowed 2026-09-10   The fleet_id retirement is one sentence, not a slogan: no row carries the fleet it belongs to — the register constitutes membership. It was never a column; it was a proposal in the resolver (§13, “cheap insurance”), so retiring it means declining to add one. Three things it does not mean: facet identifiers stay (an Organisation, a Cohort and a Site each need one — they vary per member, which is the whole point of a facet); a peer link needs none, because the peer-ness lives in the routing target, not in a column on our row; and it is conditional. The moment rows from more than one installation share a table — a hosted control plane, or an FLS living in a shared cloud database — the discriminator is needed and its name is installation_id. The decision therefore has a deadline rather than a verdict: it must be settled before the FLS writes its first row, not before it is designed. Nothing has written one yet, so deferring still costs nothing today.

12Ruled2026-09-10 · the seam

The FLS holds what dies when the deployment dies

The FLS holds what dies when the deployment dies. The declared map holds what does not.Mig, 2026-09-10 — the seam between the two stores

The question that forced it was are facets limited to one FLS? Their extent is — a facet classifies the members of one register — but the things they name outlive any deployment: a group running one installation on‑premises and one in the cloud is one organisation with two registers. So extent and ownership are different questions, and only the first was ever bounded by the store.

And the reason underneath is better than the test. Sister‑3 re-read her own registry paper rather than answering from memory of it and found the real collision: that paper is scoped per operator — and this deck dropped operator scope. Her store's premise had not been contradicted, it had dissolved. Which turns the seam from a judgement into a fact:

The FLS is per‑installation. The declared map spans installations. The seam is a scope boundary, and a fact belongs to whichever scope it is true at.sister-3, 2026-09-10 — and the ruling above is the working heuristic downstream of it
FactDies with the deployment?Home
Routing · the served setYes — rebuild elsewhere and every row is wrong FLS
Substrate credentialsYes — they belong to this deployment's substrate FLS
Organisations · CohortsNo — the same organisation exists on the other side of the rebuildDeclared map
GrantsNo — but see the caveatDeclared map*

Two places this could still bite, named rather than smoothed over. *Grants may already have a third home — the identity substrate holds who may operate what, so the ruling may make the registry's grant section redundant rather than relocated, which is a better outcome but a different edit. And Site straddles: a site outlives a rebuild (the declared map), while the presences at it do not (the FLS). The test still sorts them, but only if you ask it of the presence rather than of the place.

Already built on it (ad8a55f0). The served set moved — she had proposed it in her own first slice and conceded it: “which ventures does THIS installation serve” is per‑installation by construction. What stayed with the tenant is the rule: showcase resolves through the venture's access policy rather than a how column re‑deciding visibility in a second store. One writer per fact. And if the set must ever survive a rebuild — re‑derive it, never copy it; a copy is a second source of truth and the stale one wins whenever the primary is missing.

Answered by the same letter   What identifies a facet value across two installations? Flat facets already do — the vocabulary is the identifier, so commercial=pro means the same in both registers. The org tree did not: its node id is a locally minted surrogate doing a business id's job, so two installations holding the same group had no way to say so. Now org_ref, declared and never inferred, absent meaning distinct.
